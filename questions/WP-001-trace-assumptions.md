# WP-001: assumptions made while writing the first records and the builder

Records affected: R-001 to R-014, D-001 to D-014, I-001 to I-010, WP-001.

1. **Status of the requirements.** Landscape section 6 gives the requirements no status. Assumption: `accepted`, because the binding decisions A1 to A14 derive from them. Consequence: an edit to an R- record makes its decisions "accepted and stale", which the `trace` CI job reports as a warning. If they should be `proposed` instead, change the `status` field and restamp.
2. **Which requirements each decision serves.** The plan does not say which requirements each decision serves, except for A9 (cited by R11 and R12) and A10 (cited by R13). The `derived_from` lists of the other D- records are the transcriber's reading of the plan, and each record says so in its `links_note`. D-014 (merge policy) derives from no requirement: it serves the process premise of open-items F, which has no R- record.
3. **Language of the tooling.** `overview.md` section 4 item 2 and factory section 3d suggest Python or shell, so that the overseer can run the builder in any sandbox. WP-001 specifies Julia in the `scripts/` environment, and the builder follows WP-001. An overseer without Julia reads the `trace/build/graph.json` that the builder writes. The open item in the overview is left as written.
4. **Staleness through a reviewed record.** A record that is stale only because something upstream of it is stale stops being stale when that upstream record is re-reviewed without a content change (`stamp.jl --review`). If the review changes the upstream record's content, its hash changes and the downstream records become directly stale. The alternative would require every record in the downstream chain to be re-reviewed, even when nothing it derives from changed.
5. **Interface records.** Section 0 names the roles and the rules of the interfaces, but not their signatures. Each I- record has an `open` field that says what is missing.
