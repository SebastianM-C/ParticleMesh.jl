# WP-087: choices of the pulse-shape study

The owner asked for a pulse-shape optimization with the plasma fixed, over a wavefront of aberration modes and the spectral phase, with the laser energy fixed. These are the choices the study made, each with the answer or the assumption it proceeded on. Files: `scripts/optim/pulse2d_case.jl`, `scripts/optim/pulse2d_opt.jl`.

1. **Temporal knobs.** Whether "pulse shape" includes the spectral phase. Answered by the owner: yes, GDD and TOD are free next to the wavefront. At a fixed spectrum the GDD is the duration knob, so the duration itself stays fixed.
2. **Near field.** Whether the near field's amplitude is a parameter. Answered by the owner: it is fixed, a super-Gaussian flat-top, and the optimization is over phases only. Then the energy through the pupil is the same at every point and a₀ is a constant of the study.
3. **How many modes.** Answered by the owner: radial order 4 to start with. In 2D that is the Legendre modes up to P₄. Tilt (P₁) only steers the beam and defocus (P₂) moves the focus, which the focus parameter already does, so the free modes are P₃ (the coma-like mode, odd in y) and P₄ (the spherical-like mode, even). Assumed: the same reading of order 4 holds for the 3D half (OSA j 3 to 14 without tilts and defocus).
4. **Starts.** Answered by the owner: several random starts. A mode odd under a mirror of the box that keeps the polarization has zero gradient at an unaberrated start, so a solver started there never moves it. In 2D the polarization lies in the plane, so the mirror y → −y also flips E_y, which is the carrier phase moved by π: it pairs (P₃, φ) with (−P₃, φ + π), and P₃ does have a gradient at the unaberrated pulse (T-1184). In 3D with E along y, modes odd in z keep the zero gradient, and a mode odd in y alone is paired with the opposite carrier phase, as in 2D. The starts are drawn at random inside the bounds from a counter-based hash with a fixed seed, so they are the same on every machine. The unaberrated point is evaluated once as a reference and is not a start.
5. **Which plasma.** Assumed: WP-065's case at its base density 0.072 n_c, with the delay at 54 and the energy of the a₀ = 1.9 budget at the base Gaussian pulse (waist 10, duration 6).
6. **What the near field is.** Assumed: a super-Gaussian of order 4 whose unaberrated focus has the intensity FWHM of the base Gaussian (waist 10). Its edge is 5·10⁻⁵ of the peak at the aperture, so the hard aperture adds little ringing. The flat-top's Fresnel ringing at the antenna plane still reaches the box's y walls at 4 to 18 % of the peak field (0.4 to 3.5 % of the power falls outside the absorbers' rows), against 4·10⁻⁴ for the Gaussian. Assumed: the case's box stays 128 rows high, as in WP-065 and WP-068, since a box 1.5 times as high changes J by −2.3 to +1.2 % at four points at λ₀/16 (`launched_fraction`); a lower super-Gaussian order or a higher box would remove it. The value of the numerical aperture is in `PCASE`.
7. **The 3D half's modes and starts.** Assumed: OSA j = 3 and 5 to 14 (eleven modes; tilts and defocus left out as in 2D), each in waves of unit rms, with the GDD and the TOD free as in 2D: fourteen parameters (`scripts/optim/pulse3d_case.jl`). The near field is the 2D pilot's super-Gaussian made round, with the numerical aperture (0.371536) that gives the round base Gaussian's focal FWHM. Zernike modes are orthogonal over the uniform disk, not over this flat-top, whose amplitude falls off before the rim. There 0.1 waves of Z₁₂ alone move the on-axis peak by about 45 c/ω₀, so the spherical mode and the focus would trade for each other along a near-degenerate direction. The case's j = 12 is therefore Z₁₂ − α·Z₄ with α (−1.44) the amplitude-weighted least-squares defocus in Z₁₂ (`spherical_balance`), which keeps the peak within 2 c/ω₀ of the focus (T-1230). Coma is not balanced against tilt: 0.1 waves of Z₇ or Z₈ steer the focal spot by 3.8 c/ω₀, a third of its FWHM, which the study keeps as part of the mode. The starts draw the focus, the GDD and the TOD over the box like the 2D starts, but every mode only within ±0.05 waves, which gives Strehl ratios of 0.81 to 0.96 over the first eight starts. Eleven modes drawn over their whole box give 0.35 to 0.82 (0.63 on average over eight draws), where less is trapped, and the starts' purpose is only to move every mode off the mirror-symmetric zero.
8. **The 3D modes' bound.** Assumed: ±0.1 waves per mode. Eleven modes at their bounds are a wavefront of about a third of a wave rms, whose wings leave the 128-cell cross-section. Over all 4096 sign patterns at focus 64 and 110 at λ₀/16, the worst (focus 110) drives 88.5 % of the energy inside the transverse absorbers, the median 95 %; the same pattern gives 92 % at ±0.08, 84 % at ±0.12 and 78 % at ±0.15. The unaberrated pulse gives 99.2 % at focus 80 and 98 % at 110, and the first eight starts 97.4 to 99.4 %. No per-mode box keeps every corner of eleven modes near 1, so every point records its launched share and the report gives it at each optimum; a constraint on the total rms (not a box) would be the alternative.
9. **The 3D gradient and check.** Assumed: λ₀/16 in Float32 on the RTX 5090 with forward duals in chunks of four partials (the Dual{4} run of the 3D case fits the card, WP-084), so a point is four runs with duals. The check uses central differences of Float32 plain runs on the same numerics along the gradient's direction and two random directions at two steps, since fourteen Float64 directions at this size would take days on that card.
