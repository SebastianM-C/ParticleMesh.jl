## 1. Setup

WP-018 brings the engine to `N = 2` (grid, guards, shapes, gather, deposit, sort, position advance, loading) and keeps `N = 1` bit for bit. It ran in three phases: (1) digests of every `N = 1` entry point and the mechanism measurement on prototype kernels, both committed before any change to `lib/ParticleMeshCore/src`; (2) the port with the chosen mechanism; (3) the measurements on the ported code. The contracts it settles are restated for `N` in `docs/plan/architecture-draft.md` §0.7.

Timing method (`scripts/engine2d/common.jl`, `timed`): one warm-up call, then 7 samples, each the mean of 5 calls; the median is reported with the minimum and maximum of the samples. Particles per particle-count: 64 per cell, offsets drawn from a fixed LCG, sorted by linear cell.

## 2. The kernel mechanism (phase 1, prototypes)

`scripts/engine2d/timing.jl prototypes` times three prototype 2D Reference deposition kernels, each one work item per node, the stencil's cells in increasing column-major linear index and within a cell in slot order:

- `cartesian`: a `CartesianIndices` loop over the per-axis cell ranges of the node's stencil;
- `valtuple`: per-axis tuples with the loop nest generated by recursion on the tuple length (`Val{N}`-style, generic in N);
- `nested`: explicit nested per-axis loops for N = 2;

against a prototype 2D Fast (atomic) deposition and the engine's 1D Reference and Fast deposition at the same particle count (192 or 3072 cells of 64 particles), for orders 1 and 2, the natural stagger pair (order 1 at (Node, Node), order 2 at (Center, Center)) and the other one, Float32 and Float64, 16 × 12 and 64 × 48 cells.

The three prototypes give bitwise equal results in every case (`bitwise_equal_cartesian` in the sidecars), and the prototype Fast deposition agrees with them to 0 relative difference at 1 thread.

Geometric mean over the 16 cases of each mechanism's time relative to the fastest of the three in that case, and the number of cases it was fastest:

| mechanism | 1 thread | 4 threads |
|---|---|---|
| `cartesian` | 1.03 (fastest in 10/16) | 1.11 (8/16) |
| `nested` | 1.08 (5/16) | 1.05 (6/16) |
| `valtuple` | 1.15 (1/16) | 1.26 (2/16) |

**Choice: `CartesianIndices`.** `cartesian` and `nested` tie within the run-to-run spread (each is fastest in about half the cases, the order of the two geometric means flips between the thread counts, and single cases move by more than the difference, figure `prototypes_t1.png`); `valtuple` is slower in both. Between the two that tie, `CartesianIndices` is generic in `N` and is the §0.7 proposal, so the port uses it.

The 2D Reference deposition relative to the 1D Reference deposition and to the 2D Fast deposition (geometric means of the `cartesian` prototype over the 8 cases of each stagger kind):

| | 2D Ref / 1D Ref, 1 thread | 4 threads | 2D Ref / 2D Fast, 1 thread | 4 threads |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| natural staggers | 3.13 | 2.45 | 0.42 | 0.59 |
| other staggers | 4.84 | 3.97 | 1.95 | 1.99 |

ns per particle at 64 × 48 cells, 1 thread (the full table is in `prototypes_t{1,4}.json`):

| T, order, stagger | `cartesian` | `nested` | `valtuple` | 2D Fast | 1D Ref | 1D Fast |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Float32, 1, natural | 7.3 | 7.5 | 9.7 | 23.6 | 3.4 | 13.9 |
| Float32, 2, natural | 25.9 | 29.3 | 29.9 | 50.7 | 5.7 | 19.8 |
| Float32, 2, other | 121 | 123 | 120 | 55.1 | 18.3 | 19.5 |
| Float64, 2, natural | 25.4 | 28.3 | 30.9 | 51.4 | 7.8 | 18.2 |

At the natural staggers the Reference path, which recomputes each particle's weights for every node of its stencil (9 nodes at order 2, 4 at order 1), is still cheaper than the CPU atomics; at the other staggers the `floor` of the offset widens the stencil to 4 cells per axis at order 2 (16 cells per node), and Reference costs about twice Fast. The per-node recomputation is the same pattern #93 reports for the 1D flux deposition.

## 3. The ported deposition (phase 3)

`scripts/engine2d/timing.jl ported` times the engine's 2D `deposit!` in both modes next to the `cartesian` prototype, in the same process, on the same sorted particles (the engine's `sort_by_cell!` reproduces the host-built segment bounds, asserted). The ported Reference deposition is bitwise equal to the prototype in all 16 cases at both thread counts. Its time relative to the prototype, geometric mean over the 16 cases (range of the cases), in two runs per thread count:

| | run 1 (`…T025747`, `…T025904`) | run 2 (`…T035723`, `…T035838`) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 thread | 1.07 (0.74 to 1.76) | 1.14 (0.85 to 1.43) |
| 4 threads | 1.12 (0.77 to 1.59) | 1.10 (0.82 to 1.59) |

Single cases move between runs by more than these means (run 1's 1.76, Float64 order 2 other staggers at 16 × 12, came from a slow stretch: its samples sit at [252, 265] ns against [140, 153] for the prototype in the same run, and the same case measures 137 against 129 ns in run 2). The four means all lie above one, so the ported kernel costs about 10 % more than the prototype it reproduces bitwise. The cause is not isolated: the ported kernel computes the same products in the same order, but through the generic per-axis helpers (`linear_cell`, `tensor_pick`, `particle_offset`) rather than the prototype's hand-written N = 2 expressions. The mechanism's cost relative to the alternatives (section 2) is unaffected by an overhead of this size, which is below the spread between `cartesian` and `nested`; a per-kernel study is part of the follow-up (#135).

## 4. Scratch per particle and per cell

`Workspace` of a 2D species (`scratch_t1.json`), at 16 × 12 cells with 6 per cell and 64 × 48 cells with 64 per cell:

| | per particle | per linear cell |
|---|---|---|
| Float32, no extras | 33 B | 8 B |
| Float32, extras `(old_cell = Int32, old_offset = T)` | 33 B | 8 B |
| Float64, no extras | 37 B | 8 B |

The per-particle scratch equals the 1D figure (`reports/spike-electrostatic-1d/`, 33 B for Float32): one permutation buffer per element type serves both axes, and the two extras reuse the core buffers. The per-cell part is the two `Int32` segment bounds.

## 5. The engine-only proxy step (phase 3)

Advance (per axis, through `advance`), sort (Reference only), order-2 deposit at (Center, Center), fold, fill, order-2 gather; 16 × 12 cells; 50 steps per sample, 7 samples; ns per particle-step, median [min, max] of run 1 (`proxy_t{1,4}.json`); the median of run 2 after the semicolon (`proxy_t{1,4}_run2.json`):

| | 2 × 3 per cell (1152 particles), 1 thread | 4 threads | 8 × 8 per cell (12 288), 1 thread | 4 threads |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Float32, Reference | 543 [490, 595]; 558 | 1246 [1015, 1431]; 1241 | 154 [142, 173]; 168 | 189 [145, 299]; 193 |
| Float32, Fast | 270 [250, 291]; 272 | 419 [401, 454]; 394 | 107 [102, 127]; 118 | 204 [187, 214]; 190 |
| Float64, Reference | 543 [514, 584]; 545 | 1135 [1021, 1263]; 1122 | 176 [165, 257]; 161 | 219 [190, 296]; 189 |
| Float64, Fast | 267 [234, 359]; 259 | 404 [392, 457]; 407 | 106 [97, 116]; 110 | 171 [168, 182]; 161 |

At the CI size of 1152 particles the step is dominated by the fixed cost of its ten or so kernel launches (and of AcceleratedKernels' sort), which is why 4 threads are slower than 1 and why the cost per particle-step falls by a factor of three to 64 particles per cell. These are CPU figures for the test budget (`questions/wp-018-2d-cpu-budget.md`), not throughput figures: A7's throughput budget is on the GPU. A study at larger sizes is a follow-up issue.

## 7. N = 1 before and after

- **Engine digests (T-260).** `lib/ParticleMeshCore/test/n1_digests.jl` holds 81 FNV digests of the `N = 1` outputs of `load_quiet!`, `sort_by_cell!`, `deposit!` (both modes, orders 1 and 2, both staggers), `gather!`, `fill_guards!`, `fold_guards!`, `advance` and `deposit_flux!` (Reference), per element type, recorded on `89fbe78`'s code at 1 and 4 threads (identical, the Fast lines included). After the port they are reproduced in process and in the subprocesses at 1 and 4 threads; T-136's subprocess digests and T-170's recorded values are unchanged.
- **The module's T-218 digests.** `scripts/engine2d/n1_bits.jl` ran the module's determinism worker at 1 and 4 threads: the 18 digests (3 cases × 3 element types × 2 thread counts) are equal before (`a9e63dc`) and after (`62b9dda`) the port (`n1_digests_t{1,4}_{before,after}.json`).
- **The 1D timings** of `scripts/es1d/timing.jl`'s configurations (3 repeats of 50 steps, `n1_timing_t{1,4}_{before,after}.json`). The after/before ratio of the medians has a geometric mean of 1.08 at 1 thread and 1.04 at 4 threads; the ranges of the repeats overlap in 12 of 16 configurations at 1 thread and 14 of 16 at 4 threads. The runs were 50 minutes apart. A supplementary check, interleaving the two source trees (pre-port `src`, ported `src`, twice each, 1 thread, ppc 256 to 4096, 3 repeats each; a scratch run without a manifest, not committed) gives an after/before geometric mean of 1.01 (configurations from 0.89 to 1.16), while the two pre-port rounds alone differ by 6 %. The `N = 1` kernels are unchanged and their bits equal; the timings are unchanged within the run-to-run spread of this VM.
